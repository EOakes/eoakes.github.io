About me

I'm a PhD candidate at the University of Connecticut, researching the structure of the interstellar medium (clouds of gas and dust in space) and its relation to star formation. As part of the PHANGS and sSCALES collaborations, my work focuses on nearby, massive, star-forming galaxies like the Sculptor Galaxy (NGC 253) and NGC 4945, which we reveal at high resolution using telescopes like ALMA, JWST, and HST.

I'm the PI of two ALMA programs targeting NGC 4945: "Revealing the vertical structure of molecular gas in a nearby Milky Way analog" and "The survival and evolution of high-altitude molecular gas in a unique nuclear outflow."

My expertise includes the structure and properties of cloud-like objects in molecular gas, as well as data reduction for the PHANGS-ALMA and PHANGS-JWST pipelines. I'm also highly involved in several community outreach and advocacy projects including the UConn STARs program, Skype a Scientist, the PHANGS YouTube and PHANGS colloquium series, and UConn's Graduate Employee and Postdoc Union.

You can read more about my research, publications, and outreach on this page.

Outreach & teaching

Rather than imposing knowledge or charity from the top down, I believe in empowering students and minoritized groups to learn, do science, and advocate for themselves. I'm proud to carry out this philosophy in my teaching, undergraduate mentorship, and work with the STARs program. I'm also a strong believer in science communication, which I support through volunteering with Skype a Scientist and coordinating several outreach efforts for the PHANGS team.

Teaching

When teaching, I strive to remain approachable, engaged, and promote hands-on learning. I've led classes as a course assistant and teaching assistant for several lab-based introductory astronomy and physics courses at UConn. These include multiple sections of Introductory Astronomy with Laboratory (PHYS 1025Q), General Physics with Calculus I (1401Q), and Physics for Engineers II (1502Q). If you're a student looking to reach me, find my office hours and contact info below.

Mentoring

From 2024-2026, I helped guide Lia Gilmore through her undergraduate thesis project studying the resolved impact of AGN feedback on molecular gas properties. Lia presented this work at the 247th AAS meeting in Phoenix, Arizona and is now a masters student at CUNY. In 2022, I mentored Neal Krishna through his honors thesis project, "A pixel-to-pixel analysis of the Kennicutt-Schmidt relationship in NGC 4254." Neal presented this research at the American Astronomical Society's 241st meeting in Seattle, Washington, and graduated to a Fulbright scholarship in Taiwan.

STARs

The UConn Science Technology and Astronomy Recruits (STARs) program, started by Prof. Battersby and students in 2022, aims to recruit and retain undergraduate students from historically excluded groups in Physics. I am a managing co-lead of the program, working with the cohort to organize professional development workshops, social and community-building events, and physics lessons at local schools. I also led the development and publication of a "BiteSci" lesson plan for high school classrooms.

Skype a Scientist

Through Skype a Scientist, I'm connected with classrooms, families, and student groups around the world to talk about astrophysics. I've given in-person and virtual presentations (including about the lunar phases and eclipses), guided student projects (including about Mars colonization), answered questions, and discussed my research with many different groups. If you're interested in a call or visit, get in touch!

PHANGS YouTube & Colloquia

Sharing the awesome research happening in the PHANGS collaboration is an essential part of our duty as scientists. With a few other PHANGS members, I founded our YouTube channel in 2023 for public-facing videos about recent discoveries and publications. Additionally, I founded a monthly PHANGS colloquium series to keep the collaboration informed about cross-discipline research and build early-career members' presentation skills.

Union Organizing

A positive working environment is one where quality research thrives! I served as the Union Guide from 2023-24, an elected member on the executive board of UConn's Graduate Employee and Postdoc Union (GEU-UAW Local 6950). As a current member of the Organizing Committee and Grievance Committee, I work to ensure that our members are treated fairly and equitably by the University. If you have any questions about the Union, want to get involved, or have concerns about your working conditions, please get in touch!
